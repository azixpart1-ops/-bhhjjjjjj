## Recurring phrases

- **loves it / absolutely loves it / loves his (her) new bed** By far the most repeated construction — appears in virtually every 5-star review across all 8 brands (Omlet, Orvis, Charley Chau, Collared Creatures, Scruffs, Lords & Labradors, Big Barker). Almost never 'I love it'; the verdict is always given in the dog's name.
- **straight in it / jumped straight on it / as soon as it was unwrapped (opened / out of the box)** 4+ independent instances (Charley Chau, Omlet x2, Orvis). The single most repeated NARRATIVE beat: the first 60 seconds. This is the moment owners treat as proof the money was well spent.
- **the only bed he/she will actually sleep on** 3+ instances (Omlet 'the only dog bed I have bought that my dog will actually sleep on', 'ignored every single bed we've bought', Orvis 'we've tried a lot of dog beds over the years'). Implies a graveyard of failed beds behind the purchase.
- **settled / settled down / so settled and happy** Recurs across Omlet reviews. In UK usage 'settled' is the high compliment — it means the pacing, circling and re-settling has stopped.
- **sleeps through the night / sleeps really well / sleeping soundly** 3 instances across Omlet, Orvis. Framed as something previously thought impossible ('unheard of for springers').
- **still going strong / still in perfect condition / no sign of ware / kept its shape / still as firm as the day I got it** 5+ instances (Charley Chau, Collared Creatures x2, Orvis, Omlet, Big Barker). Always paired with an exact time span — 8yrs, 9 years, 5 years, a year, several years.
- **well made / good quality / excellent quality / quality is second to none / a well made bed** Near-universal. UK buyers lead with construction quality before comfort.
- **worth every penny / money very well spent / worth the price / not worth £135** Value is stated explicitly, in pounds, in both directions. Positive and negative reviews both do the arithmetic out loud.
- **out of choice** A quietly powerful recurring phrase — 'she is better now and still uses her bed out of choice'. The dog electing the bed once it no longer has to is the strongest possible proof.
- **struggle up from the ground / get on and off / step off it** Mobility is described physically and concretely, never as 'joint health'. Berkeley, Omlet.
- **rest his head** 2 independent instances (Omlet Helen, Lords & Labradors Vivienne) describing the bolster. Owners buy bolsters specifically as a pillow, not as a wall.
- **washable / easy to wash / wash the cover / removable cover** Mentioned in almost every positive review AND weaponised in negatives ('major task putting the cover back on', 'looks absolutely awful after washing'). Washing is the daily reality test.
- **looks great in my living room / in keeping with our furniture / a classy dog bed / looks gorgeous** 5+ instances. The bed is judged as furniture by a partner or visitor, not only as a dog product.
- **The dog's first name, used immediately** Dave, Skye, Buster, Lola, Bella, Bailey. Owners name the dog in the first sentence. Generic 'my dog' is the fallback; 'pet parent' and 'fur baby' appear nowhere in any UK review fetched.
- **Really pleased / very pleased / pleased with** The default British opener for a positive review. Understated, not superlative.

## Themes

### The first 60 seconds — instant adoption

What owners say: 'As soon as I got this bed out, she jumped straight on it!' (Omlet); 'He was straight in it for a snooze as soon as it was unwrapped' (Charley Chau); 'From the moment I brought it out of the box and laid it on the floor, he has claimed this bed for his own!!' (Orvis); 'Dogs couldn't wait to get into their new bed' (Omlet).

Why it matters: This is the single most repeated story shape across every brand. Buyers of an £80-£200 bed are frightened the dog will ignore it. Open descriptions by scripting that first moment ('Unroll it. Nine times out of ten they're on it before you've folded the box') rather than leading with foam density. It also directly de-risks the 100-night trial — pair the two.

### Sleeping through the night — the owner's night, not just the dog's

What owners say: 'she sleeps on it through the night (unheard of for springers)' (Omlet); 'she has been sleeping soundly since it arrived' (Orvis); 'They sleep really well on it, it is supportive' (Omlet); 'My dog sleeps super hard on this and loves the support' (Omlet).

Why it matters: The unspoken benefit is that the OWNER gets to sleep. Reviews reference being woken repeatedly before the bed. For PawLunova's memory foam and bolster lines, write the outcome as a night: the clicking nails on the floor at 3am stopping, the re-settling stopping. Use 'settled' and 'through the night' — their words.

### Getting up in the morning — mobility described physically

What owners say: 'The height and firmness of the bed allows our lab to step off it rather than struggle up from the ground' (Berkeley); 'She was able to get on and off it with a cone on' (Omlet); 'at 9 years old, he's starting to feel the effects of an overly exuberant youth' (Omlet).

Why it matters: Owners never say 'orthopaedic support'. They describe a specific physical motion — stepping off versus hauling themselves up off a flat floor. Rewrite spec claims as motion: what the dog does differently at 6am. Firmness + entry height is the mechanism they actually understand and value. Note this is also the honest, non-medical way to make the claim (UK: avoid implying treatment of arthritis).

### The graveyard of rejected beds

What owners say: 'My Irish Terrier has ignored every single bed we've bought for her' (Omlet); 'This is the only dog bed I have bought that my dog will actually sleep on!' (Omlet); 'We've tried a lot of dog beds over the years and the Orvis memory foam bolster is the favorite' (Orvis); 'she's using it more often than any other dog bed in the house' (Omlet).

Why it matters: The PawLunova buyer has already wasted money on two or three cheap beds. Acknowledging that sunk cost — naming the pile of rejected beds in the utility room — earns instant recognition and reframes £149 as the end of the cycle rather than another gamble.

### Longevity is how they justify the price to themselves and their partner

What owners say: 'the 1st lasted my dog 8yrs' (Collared Creatures); '9 years later, he STILL sleeps on it every day!... the foam is still as firm and supportive as the day I got it. No permanent indents or sinking areas' (Orvis); 'I ordered the x-large bed about 5 years ago and it is still the best bed I have ever bought' (Big Barker); 'both are still going strong' (Charley Chau); 'it's the only bed your dog will ever need' (Big Barker).

Why it matters: Always with a number of years attached. This is the direct hook for the 5-year no-flatten guarantee — express it the way customers express it ('still the same shape', 'no dip where he lies'), and do the cost-per-year arithmetic in the copy, because customers visibly do it themselves.

### Washing is the daily reality test — and the most common hidden failure

What owners say: 'It is super easy to put on, clean and wash' (Omlet); 'we can wash the cover' (Orvis); but also 'It is a major task putting the cover back on though' (Omlet, inside a 10/10); 'The material looks absolutely awful after washing it correctly' (Charley Chau 1★); 'Even the cover doesn't fit the bed fully' (Scruffs 1★).

Why it matters: Even delighted customers complain about refitting the cover. Specify the wash temperature, confirm it goes back on easily (full-length zip, cover cut to the foam), and state it survives repeated washing. This is a cheap, high-credibility differentiator competitors are visibly failing.

### The bed is furniture — it has to pass the living room test

What owners say: 'it looks in keeping with our furniture' (Omlet); 'It looks great in my living room. A classy dog bed' (Omlet); 'Our girl loves her new bed and it looks gorgeous in our living room' (Omlet); 'Very stylish for a pet sofa' (Omlet).

Why it matters: There is a second decision-maker in the house who has to agree it can sit in the lounge. Describe how it looks in a room — colour against a sofa, height, whether it reads as a dog bed — not just how it performs. This is under-served at PawLunova's price point.

### Security and burrowing — the nest/cave buyer is buying calm, not comfort

What owners say: 'We've had the bed less than 24 hours and we have only seen our GSP come out of her bed to eat or go outside!' (Collared Creatures); 'My dog absolutely loves his snuggle sack' (Collared Creatures); 'It's become his favourite place to snuggle up and nap' (Scruffs); 'when she lays in her Orvis bed she is completely relaxed and content' (Orvis, anxious rescue dog).

Why it matters: For the nest and cave lines the outcome word is not 'comfort', it is 'relaxed', 'content', 'settled', 'his own place'. Write to the anxious dog and the owner who feels responsible for that anxiety.

### Post-surgery and recovery — an under-exploited buying occasion

What owners say: 'she had major surgery and couldn't jump on and off the sofa' (Omlet); 'She was able to get on and off it with a cone on' (Omlet); 'She is better now and still uses her bed out of choice' (Omlet).

Why it matters: A distinct, urgent, high-intent purchase trigger nobody in this set is writing to directly. Worth naming explicitly in low-entry / waterproof / memory foam descriptions and in SEO ('dog bed after surgery', 'dog bed for recovery', 'low sided dog bed cone'). Also strong for the waterproof line.

### The bolster is a pillow, not a wall

What owners say: 'The raised areas allow him to rest his head without stretching this vulnerable area' (Omlet); 'A nice place for him to rest his head' (Lords & Labradors); 'the bolster provides the perfect cushion for them to snuggle into' (Omlet); 'The bolster is an amazing pillow for my dog' (Omlet).

Why it matters: Owners independently arrive at the word 'pillow' and the idea of head/neck support. Bolster product copy should lead with head-resting, not with 'raised sides for security'.

### Chew resistance as relief

What owners say: 'The big bonus is that he hasn't tried to destroy it' (Charley Chau); 'my dogs have not been able to break into any of them' (Charley Chau).

Why it matters: A recurring quiet fear at this price: spending £150 on something the dog destroys in a week. Address it plainly where the fabric genuinely supports the claim.

## Objections

- **Sizing — the single most common complaint across every brand. Beds arrive too small (or too big), and stated measurements are disputed.** — 'Poor quality product, too small for male whippet' (Charley Chau 1★); 'found it to be 4 inches short on the width' (Charley Chau 1★); 'The XL Ellen donut dog bed I ordered was great but was too big' (Scruffs 1★); 'twice i habe ordered, unfortunately underestimated the size and had to exchange' (Lords & Labradors 1★). FIX: publish internal sleeping-surface dimensions (not just external), a breed-and-weight size chart, and a measure-your-dog instruction in every description.
- **Foam flattens, or is lumpy — and 'orthopaedic' is suspected of being a marketing word with nothing behind it.** — 'it's so lumpy, I don't know how it can be called orthopaedic. After one use I can see visible flattening' (Scruffs 1★). FIX: this is exactly what the 5-year no-flatten guarantee answers. State foam density/depth as a number and let the guarantee carry the risk, in the buyer's own language: 'still the same shape'.
- **Cover fails — hard to refit after washing, shrinks, looks ruined, or fades.** — 'It is a major task putting the cover back on though' (Omlet, inside a 10/10); 'The material looks absolutely awful after washing it correctly' (Charley Chau 1★); 'Even the cover doesn't fit the bed fully, it's too big and moves all the time' (Scruffs 1★); '18 months and it's completely faded! Not a little, a complete change in colour' (Scruffs 1★). FIX: state wash temperature, full-perimeter zip, colourfastness, and that the cover is cut to the foam.
- **The price is not believed to be justified — buyers say the number out loud.** — 'absolutely not worth £135' (Charley Chau 1★). FIX: counter with the longevity arithmetic customers already use themselves ('8yrs', '9 years later'), cost-per-year, and the 100-night trial.
- **'Waterproof' is not trusted — buyers have been burned by the claim before.** — 'However don't get the so called waterproof lining because it's not waterproof is not worth the $80' (Big Barker owner comment, K9 of Mine). FIX: for the waterproof line, specify exactly what is waterproof (cover, inner liner, both), and what it withstands — accidents, wet dog, mud — rather than the bare word.
- **Slow or silent delivery on small premium brands, which triggers real fear about money.** — 'Still waiting for dog bed to arrive and they are not responding to my emails' and 'I'm worried where my money has gone' (Berkeley 1★); 'No communication until we chased them after almost two weeks' (Berkeley 1★). ## Style notes

HOW THESE BUYERS ACTUALLY WRITE — and how to write back to them.

SENTENCE LENGTH AND RHYTHM: Short declaratives and comma-spliced run-ons. Typical review is 1-3 sentences. 'Really pleased with these beds. I initially ordered 2, one for each dog.' They rarely use semicolons or subordinate clauses. Match this: short sentences, one idea each. Long, flowing brand-voice paragraphs read as inauthentic to this audience.

THE VERDICT IS ALWAYS THE DOG'S: The grammatical subject is the dog, not the product and not the owner. 'She sleeps on it through the night.' 'He has claimed this bed for his own.' 'They've not got a look in.' Never 'you'll love the supportive foam' — write 'she stops circling and just lies down'. Product benefits must be converted into observable dog behaviour.

VOCABULARY THEY USE: settled, snuggle, snooze, comfy, cosy, well made, quality, sturdy, supportive, straight on it, out of choice, rest his head, stretch out, den, still going strong, worth every penny, really pleased, chuffed, spoilt. British spelling throughout: orthopaedic (never orthopedic), colour, favourite, cosy, metres/cm.

VOCABULARY THEY NEVER USE: 'pet parent', 'fur baby', 'furry friend' (zero instances in any UK review fetched), 'revolutionary', 'cutting-edge', 'premium orthopaedic support system', 'ergonomically engineered', 'elevates your dog's sleep experience', 'game-changing'. They also never use clinical language — no 'joint pathology', no 'pressure redistribution'. They say 'struggle up from the ground'.

THEY QUANTIFY, ALWAYS: '8yrs', '9 years later', '5 years ago', '18 months', 'less than 24 hours', '4 inches short', '£135', '£68'. Numbers are how they establish credibility with each other. Copy should carry specific numbers — foam depth in cm, years of guarantee, nights of trial, internal dimensions — not vague superlatives.

THEY NAME THE DOG: Dave, Skye, Buster, Lola, Bella. Often the breed too — springer, whippet, GSP, collie, lab, Frenchie, Irish Terrier, Golden Retriever, dachsie. Breed-specific and size-specific language lands hard, and is also strong long-tail SEO ('dog bed for greyhound', 'large dog bed for labrador').

TONE: Understated, not hyped. The British default opener is 'Really pleased' or 'Very happy'. When they do go big it's with capitals and exclamation marks, not adjectives — 'HE LOVES IT!!', 'STILL sleeps on it every day!'. So: let the enthusiasm come from the specificity of the story, not from adjective stacking.

THEY WRITE WITH TYPOS AND DON'T CARE: 'no sign of ware', 'amd', 'habe', 'Daves bed', 'lined them so much'. Do NOT imitate typos in your copy — but do imitate the unpolished directness. Perfect, balanced marketing prose reads as corporate; plain, slightly blunt sentences read as true.

STRUCTURE THAT MIRRORS HOW THEY THINK (use as the description skeleton): (1) the problem they already have — the old dog struggling up, the pile of rejected beds, the 3am pacing; (2) the first-60-seconds moment; (3) the mechanism in physical terms — firmness, depth, entry height, bolster as pillow; (4) the durability proof with a number; (5) the practical objections pre-answered — sizing, washing, waterproofing; (6) the risk removal — 100 nights, 5 years.
