## Recurring phrases

- **my old boy / the old boy** Extremely common — the default affectionate term for an ageing male dog on Mumsnet and The Labrador Forum. 'My old girl' for females. Use it; do not say 'senior canine companion'.
- **struggles to get up / getting up (after rest, in the morning)** The single most repeated symptom phrase across every source, and the one most often used to report success ('he's getting up a lot easier'). Appeared in 8+ of the sources fetched.
- **back legs / back end / hind legs** Near-universal. UK owners say 'his back legs have gone' or 'arthritis in his back end'. They never say 'hindquarters' or 'rear limbs'.
- **dodgy hips / dodgy back legs** Understated British idiom for a serious problem. Appeared on Mumsnet dog-bed threads multiple times.
- **stiff / stiffness** Used by owners and by PDSA/Canine Arthritis Management identically — rare case where clinical and owner language match. Safe SEO term.
- **slipping and sliding / slippy floors / laminate** Repeated across Mumsnet arthritis threads and PDSA guidance. Non-slip base is a real, unmet buying criterion.
- **supportive / support** The benefit word they actually use — 'very supportive', 'the right level of support', 'such support'. More persuasive to them than 'orthopaedic'.
- **it's a bit spendy but worth it** The exact self-permission formula at £149. Mirror this structure rather than discounting.
- **£££ / ££££** Shorthand for 'too expensive'. Signals the price ceiling is emotional, not just numeric.
- **lasted really well / last literally forever / still like new / last bloody ages** Longevity is the highest compliment in this category — used more often than comfort. Directly answers the flattening fear.
- **doesn't sag / doesn't lose shape / went flat** The precise wording of the anti-flatten promise. A 5-year no-flatten guarantee should be worded in these terms, not 'foam integrity'.
- **washable / washes well / chuck it in the machine / unzipped, washed, dried** Named in virtually every bed recommendation thread. Often the first criterion listed, ahead of comfort.
- **waterproof / hose it down / wipe with a wet cloth** Old dogs have accidents — owners say this obliquely. Waterproof inner is a dignity feature, not just a practical one.
- **raised sides / raised edge / bolster / prop herself up against** Owners describe the bolster by what the dog does with it (propping, resting head, leaning), not by its name.
- **orthopaedic (UK 'ae' spelling)** UK buyers and UK retailers spell it 'orthopaedic'; Google UK serves both. Use 'orthopaedic' in body copy and cover 'orthopedic' in meta/keywords for the US-spelling searches.
- **memory foam** The single most-typed product term in these threads — more than 'orthopaedic'. Should lead Google Shopping titles.
- **sore joints / achy joints** Owner-side wording for arthritis. 'Ease her sore joints' outperforms 'joint support system'.
- **he loves it / she absolutely loves it** The standard review opener. Dog approval, not owner satisfaction, is the proof they seek.
- **we can see the difference in him already** The aspirational outcome sentence. A 100-night trial should be framed as time to see the difference.
- **horrible to see them get old** The emotional register: plain, short, unadorned. Never escalate past this level of language.
- **draughts / draught-free (also misspelt 'droughts')** UK-specific cold-house concern. Supports raised beds, insulated bases and nest styles.
- **ddog / DDog** Mumsnet shorthand for 'darling dog'. Do not use in copy, but it signals how casually and affectionately they write.
- **quality of life** The phrase that appears when things get serious. Avoid it in sales copy — it is associated with euthanasia decisions.
- **Librela / Galliprant / Loxicom / Yumove / hydrotherapy** Named UK treatments. Referencing the landscape ('alongside their Librela or Yumove') signals you know this buyer. Never claim to replace them.
- **took a chance** How they describe buying from an unknown online brand. Risk-reversal (100-night trial) should answer this phrase directly.
- **kg and breed as identity** 'my 17.5kg collie', 'my 30kg lab', 'my staffy'. Sizing guidance should be given in breed + kg, not just cm.

## Themes

### Getting up after rest is THE symptom and THE proof

What owners say: 'He just really struggles to get up from lying down' (Mumsnet). 'Stiffness when getting up after a period of rest' (Canine Arthritis Management). And when a bed works: 'He's extremely comfortable on it, he's getting up a lot easier' (Tuffies) and 'it's really helped to ease the oldest sore joints when she gets up in the morning' (Mumsnet).

Why it matters: This is the before/after axis for the entire memory-foam and orthopaedic range. Every product description should open on the morning-stiffness moment and close on the easier-rise outcome, in their words. It is also the highest-intent search behaviour — build SEO around 'dog bed for arthritis getting up' and 'memory foam dog bed stiff back legs'.

### The dog abandoning or rejecting the bed is the biggest pre-purchase fear

What owners say: 'She had her bed before but she didn't like it...Now she is using her new bed' (Zooplus). 'My arthritic dog chose this bed over the available foam bed and the sofa' (Canine Arthritis Management reviewer). 'redboxer321 notes personal dog rejected it in favor of memory foam slab' (Mumsnet). Owners also test two beds and let the dog pick: 'We also gave the option of two beds...He eventually always picked the downstairs one.'

Why it matters: The £80–£200 hesitation is not 'is it good?' — it is 'will he actually use it?'. The 100-night home trial is the single strongest asset in the store and should be framed exactly as that: time for your dog to choose it. Lead with dog-chose-it proof, not feature lists.

### Slippery floors and draughts are treated as part of the same problem as the bed

What owners say: 'He's much worse on the laminate flooring' and 'staggering around and slipping and sliding' (Mumsnet). PDSA: 'Use rugs, carpets or anti-slip mats to make floors around the house less slippy. Dogs with arthritis have weak muscles, which means they are prone to slipping and struggle to get up.' Canine Arthritis Management: 'locating in a warm draft free position'. And 'the Omlet ones seem to have a thicker base and you can add legs...so it's slightly off the ground (better for droughts).'

Why it matters: Non-slip bases and insulation from cold floors are not minor specs to this buyer — they are core. Any product with a grippy base, a raised design or a thick insulating base should say so in the first three lines. It also opens a genuine differentiator most competitors bury.

### Washability and waterproofing come before comfort in the buying checklist

What owners say: 'Covers wash well, and the inner is waterproof so you can hose it down or give a thorough wipe with a wet cloth if it needs it. Last bloody ages' (Mumsnet). 'the whole thing can be unzipped, washed, dried and put back together in about 2 hours. Very user friendly!' 'Easily washable. I bought several on eBay.' 'It was cheaper than a dog bed and easier to clean.'

Why it matters: Old dogs have accidents, and owners will not say so directly — they say 'washable' and 'waterproof'. State the machine-wash temperature, whether the cover unzips fully, whether the inner is waterproof, and how fast it dries. Make this a scannable metafield block, not a buried sentence.

### Foam flattening, crumbling and failing zips are the category's trust wound

What owners say: 'foam "mattress" which shed large chunks...foam & cover flimsy' and 'zips didn't hold up at all in the wash' and 'The sides do not hold and the fleece cover kept moving...bed has ripped' (Zooplus). Conversely: 'Thick, supportive, they do not sag or loose shape', 'still like new', 'We have had the dog bed and inside cushion for 11 years'.

Why it matters: The 5-year no-flatten guarantee is the answer to the loudest complaint in the whole category — but it must be worded in their language ('won't sag, won't go flat, won't need replacing in a year'), and paired with the specific things that fail: zips, bolster collapse, cover ripping. Naming the failure you have solved is more persuasive than a generic warranty badge.

### Price band is stated openly: £100 is comfortable, £150 is the stretch

What owners say: 'could go up to £150 at a push but would prefer £100 or less if possible'. 'It's a bit spendy at £149 but worth it.' 'That Red Dog Company.... £75 for a bit of vet fleece!' 'they were ££££'. 'was suckered in by the "Black Friday" deals'.

Why it matters: This buyer audits price against material. Justify £149+ by naming what is inside (foam depth, density, cover fabric, base construction) rather than by adjectives. They are discount-aware and slightly cynical about fake sales — cost-per-night or cost-over-five-years framing will land better than a struck-through price.

### Households buy more than one bed — upstairs, downstairs, room by room

What owners say: 'memory foam bed in every room'. 'I would get a second bed for downstairs. He can then go upstairs if he feels like it but also be comfy on his downstairs bed.' 'Plans to purchase additional beds (upstairs/downstairs)...when I can afford another one.'

Why it matters: Cross-sell and repeat purchase are natural here, not pushy. Every description can close with a line about a second bed downstairs. Bundle or second-bed pricing would be welcomed, not resented.

### Sleeping style determines the product — and owners know their dog's style precisely

What owners say: 'She likes to sleep on her back so quite enjoys a raised edge to prop herself up against.' 'our dog loves it and lies on his back in it all the time.' Canine Arthritis Management: 'Some dogs prefer to curl up in a padded, easy access basket in the hustle and bustle of the home, while others want to stretch out on firm shallow cool bedding in privacy.' Tuffies: the dog 'rarely lies down in a tight curl' and 'quite often likes to stretch (rear ballerina!) lying down'.

Why it matters: Nest, bolster, flat mattress and elevated-cooling should each be positioned by sleeping style — curler vs stretcher vs back-sleeper vs hot dog — rather than by foam spec. This gives each of the 64 products a distinct, non-duplicated reason to exist, which also solves the thin-content SEO problem across a large catalogue.

### Warmth relieves pain; cooling is for a different dog

What owners say: 'consider putting your arthritic dog inside and keep him warm. Cold weather is not kind to arthritic joints' (The Labrador Forum). 'My dogs physio suggest warm coats such as equafleece, for winter months.' But also: dogs that reject beds for hard floors are 'almost certainly making a temperature choice'.

Why it matters: Do not sell the elevated-cooling range to the arthritic-dog buyer. Segment clearly: insulated, draught-free warmth for stiff old joints; elevated airflow for heavy-coated, overheating or summer dogs. Mixing the two messages loses both.

### Access height and room to reposition are specs owners feel but rarely find stated

What owners say: 'This bed is the perfect height...providing the right level of support' (Zooplus). Canine Arthritis Management: 'ensuring the bed has trip-free access and that their dog can change their position without difficulty'. PDSA: 'Lying down for hours can cause joints to become stiff, a little bit of movement every now and then can help.' Also 'This Easy Access Durasoft Nest dog bed is big enough for my dog to lie flat'.

Why it matters: Publish the step-in height in cm and the usable internal sleeping area, and say plainly that a stiff dog can step in without lifting a leg high and can turn over without scrambling. No competitor found in this research states this clearly — it is available white space.

### Guilt, shock at speed of decline, and vet-bill fatigue sit under every purchase

What owners say: 'I just feel awful that nothing seems to be doing much.' 'it's come as quite a shock how fast he's deteriorating.' 'We stupidly didn't have him insured so we are paying for all his treatment.' 'It is horrible to see them get old.' 'I just love the old boy and want him to be happy and not in pain.'

Why it matters: Do not manufacture guilt — it is already at ceiling, and pushing it will read as exploitative and drive the sale away. The winning move is relief: this is one thing you can fix today, tonight, without a vet appointment. Position the bed as the affordable, immediate, no-side-effects action in a landscape of expensive uncertain treatments.

### Proof is measured in years owned and in visible change within weeks

What owners say: 'He's only had it for about a month and we can see the difference in him already.' 'I have been using them for two years and can confirm that the high level of support.' 'We have had the dog bed and inside cushion for 11 years.' 'I got one a few years ago from Amazon, but it lasted really well up until we lost him.'

Why it matters: With zero on-site reviews, borrow the structure of credible proof rather than inventing it: a specific time window (100 nights), a specific observable change (easier to rise, settles faster, stops shifting at night), and a specific durability commitment (5 years, no flattening). Never fabricate testimonials — the objection 'took a chance' means these buyers are alert to thin social proof.

### They buy from strangers online reluctantly, and need risk reversed explicitly

What owners say: 'Looked online and found this company supplying quality memory foam dog beds at a reasonable price. Took a chance and l have to say l am extremely pleased with product, communication, packaging and delivery. Far better than anything l found in the local shops' (Trustpilot GB).

Why it matters: 'Took a chance' is the emotional state of a first-time PawLunova visitor with no reviews to read. The 100-night home trial, UK mainland delivery and the 5-year guarantee should appear high on the page and in the meta description, not in a footer. This is also a Google Shopping advantage: trial and guarantee can be surfaced in promotional and structured data.

## Style notes

HOW THEY ACTUALLY WRITE — copy this register exactly.

SENTENCE LENGTH AND SHAPE. Short, flat, declarative. 8–18 words is typical. They chain clauses with commas and ellipses rather than semicolons: "Ours is from baker and bray, it an orthopaedic bed with memory foam and padded raised edges, our dog loves it and lies on his back in it all the time .It's a bit spendy at £149 but worth it." Typos, missing apostrophes, stray spaces before full stops and lowercase brand names are normal and signal authenticity. Do not replicate errors in your copy, but do match the plainness.

VOCABULARY THEY USE. supportive, comfy, stiff, sore joints, dodgy hips, back legs, back end, gone downhill, struggles to get up, slipping and sliding, slippy, washable, waterproof, draught-free, raised sides, memory foam, orthopaedic, lasts, doesn't sag, spendy, worth it, he loves it, the old boy, my old girl, ancient. They measure dogs in kg and name the breed first ("my staffy", "my chocolate lab", "nearly 10-year old smallish collie (she's 17.5kg)").

SPELLING CONVENTIONS. British throughout: orthopaedic (not orthopedic), draught, colour, realise, metre, cosy. Note they sometimes misspell draught as "drought" and write "slippy" not "slippery". Prices always with £ and often with no decimals ("£149", "£100 or less"). Use "orthopaedic" in visible body copy and titles; carry "orthopedic" only as a secondary keyword in meta/alt text for the US-spelling search variants.

EMOTIONAL REGISTER — THIS IS THE CRITICAL CONSTRAINT. They are heavily emotional but verbally restrained. The most intense thing anyone in this research said was "It is horrible to see them get old" and "the thought of losing him at 7 is heartbreaking." That is the ceiling. They understate: "he's not as sprightly as he was", "a bit stiff", "gone downhill". Copy that out-emotes them will read as manipulative and lose the sale. The guilt is already maxed out — "I just feel awful that nothing seems to be doing much" — so the persuasive move is RELIEF, not more pressure. Position the bed as the one thing they can fix tonight, cheaply, with no side effects, while everything else (Librela, Galliprant, hydrotherapy, insurance they didn't take out) is expensive and uncertain.

WHAT THEY NEVER SAY — avoid these entirely. "Premium", "luxury", "game-changer", "revolutionary", "engineered", "cutting-edge", "pamper", "spoil your fur baby", "pawrent", "doggo", "vet-approved" without naming the vet, "clinically proven", "transform your dog's life", "say goodbye to", exclamation-heavy hype, emoji. They also never use clinical jargon — no "hindquarters", "osteoarthritic degeneration", "pressure redistribution", "spinal alignment technology". Note one genuine exception: "quality of life" is a phrase they DO use, but only in end-of-life discussions — keep it out of sales copy entirely.

WHAT PROOF LOOKS LIKE TO THEM. Time owned ("11 years", "two years", "still like new"), the dog choosing it over the sofa, and a specific observable change ("he's getting up a lot easier", "we can see the difference in him already", "eased her sore joints when she gets up in the morning"). Vague praise does nothing. With zero on-site reviews, do not fabricate testimonials — these buyers are explicitly wary ("took a chance"). Instead borrow the SHAPE of that proof: a named time window (100 nights), a named observable change, and a named durability commitment (5 years, no flattening).

STRUCTURE THAT MATCHES HOW THEY READ. They skim for: will he use it, will it wash, will it go flat, what does it cost, will it fit. Answer those five in that order. Give step-in height in cm, internal sleeping area in cm, wash temperature, whether the cover fully unzips, whether the inner is waterproof, and sizing by breed + kg — not just by S/M/L. Close each description on the second-bed idea ("most people end up wanting one downstairs too"), which they raise themselves and do not experience as an upsell.
