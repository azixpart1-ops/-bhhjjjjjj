### Google Merchant Center title rules — half of what a persuasion-first copywriter wants to do is a policy violation

Verbatim from Google's own [title] specification: title is "1–150 characters"; "Don't add information such as price, sale price, sale dates, shipping, delivery date, other time-related information, or your company's name"; "Don't use capital letters for emphasis. Best practice: "Put the most important details first. Users will usually notice only the first 70 or fewer characters"; "Use all 150 characters. Your title will be used to match your product to a customer's search"; "Add the distinguishing details of each variant to the title".

Source: https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6324415

**Action:** This directly forbids putting '100-Night Trial', 'FREE UK Delivery', '5-Year Guarantee', 'SALE', '★', or 'PawLunova' into the Shopping feed title — several of which the brief's persuasion goal would naturally reach for. Use the full 150 characters with real attributes (size in cm, foam type, cover type, waterproof, washable, colour, shape) because Google matches the query against the title. Front-load the first 70 characters.

## Hard rules

- UK spelling always: 'orthopaedic', not 'orthopedic', in every H1, title tag and Shopping feed title. Carry 'orthopedic' once in body copy or a metafield as a synonym. Same principle for 'kennel' not 'dog house', 'car boot' not 'car trunk', 'personalised' not 'personalized'.
- Never lead with the bare term 'dog bed'. Every product title must open with a qualified head term (orthopaedic / memory foam / waterproof / raised / elevated / cooling / crate / calming / donut) plus a size token. The unqualified head term puts a £49–£299 product next to a £12 Aldi bed in the same results row.
- Shopping feed titles must obey Google's [title] spec verbatim: no price, no sale text, no delivery or time-related info, no company name, no ALL CAPS for emphasis, no symbols or stars, no double spaces. That means '100-Night Trial', 'FREE UK Delivery', '5-Year Guarantee', 'SALE' and 'PawLunova' are all banned from the feed title. They belong in the description and on the landing page.
- Front-load the first 70 characters of every Shopping title with the words a buyer would actually type, then use the remaining space up to 150 characters on real attributes: size in cm, foam type, cover type, waterproof, washable, shape, colour. Structure: [Size] [feature adjectives] [material] [shape] Dog Bed [- range name].
- 'With removable, machine-washable cover' and 'waterproof' are the two most-recurring modifiers in the entire UK landscape. Put them in the first 70 characters of the title wherever they are literally true — and only where literally true.
- PawLunova has zero published reviews. Do not invent, imply or imitate any review, star rating, testimonial, review count, or review schema markup. No '4.8 stars', no 'loved by 10,000 UK dog owners', no made-up customer quotes. Fake reviews breach UK consumer law under the DMCC Act, and fake review markup is grounds for Merchant Center suspension — it would cost the Shopping placement this whole project is aiming at.
- No fake urgency or fake scarcity: no invented countdowns, 'only 3 left' where untrue, or fabricated 'X people are viewing this'. Same legal and same Merchant Center exposure as fake reviews.
- Health claims must stay descriptive, never medical. Write what the bed physically does — 'distributes weight to relieve pressure on hips and joints', 'supports the spine' — not what it cures. Never claim the bed treats, cures or relieves arthritis, hip dysplasia or IVDD. 'Designed for dogs with arthritis' is fine; 'relieves your dog's arthritis' is not.
- Avoid the absolute word 'indestructible' as a product claim even though it has search volume, unless the product genuinely carries a guarantee that backs it. Use 'tough', 'heavy-duty', 'chew-resistant', 'built for chewers'. An absolute claim on a bed that can be destroyed is a returns problem and a misleading-claim problem.
- Only claim 'Made in the UK' on products that are actually made in the UK. It is real searched demand ('dog bed uk made', 'dog bed made in uk', 'pet beds made in uk') and therefore tempting — and it is a country-of-origin claim, so a false one is a legal problem, not a copy problem.
- Every product gets a sizing metafield block: exact cm dimensions, foam depth in cm, the breeds that fit that size, and a 'measure nose-to-tail and add 15cm' instruction. Sizing uncertainty is the single biggest add-to-basket blocker at this price point and 'what size dog bed for [breed]' is the largest question cluster in the category.
- Answer the sceptical questions head-on rather than around them: does it actually work, is it worth it, what are the downsides, is memory foam safe for dogs, how do I wash it. The top-ranking questions all carry doubt ('really help', 'worth it', 'disadvantages'). Copy that ignores the doubt reads as sales patter to this buyer.
- Match the customer's register, not brochure register. Short sentences. Dog's name or 'your dog' early. Name the problem plainly — stiff after walks, struggling to settle, accidents at night, the smell — then the turn. Model: 'If your dog's starting to feel their age, gets a bit stiff after walks.' No 'furry friend', no 'pawfect', no exclamation marks.
- Persuasion comes from specificity, not pressure. The levers that actually convert this buyer are: exact foam density and depth, the 5-year no-flatten guarantee (which is also a real keyword — 'dog bed that doesn't flatten'), the 100-night trial (a conversion asset with zero search demand — keep it near the buy button, out of the title), washing instructions, and honest sizing. Emotional weight should come from accurately describing the dog's experience, never from manufactured fear, guilt-tripping the owner, or invented social proof.
- Use the buyer's plural vocabulary where it splits: crate products must carry 'crate bed', 'crate mat', 'crate mattress' and 'cage bed'; flat orthopaedic products should carry 'mattress' and 'mat' as well as 'bed'; the nest family must be called 'donut', 'calming' and 'anti-anxiety' in customer-facing copy because 'nest dog bed' has no UK search demand.
- Breed names go in body copy and metafields, never in the Shopping feed title (Google wants product attributes there). Prioritise labrador, cocker spaniel, german shepherd, dachshund, greyhound, whippet, golden retriever, great dane. Use 'Staffy' and 'Staffordshire Bull Terrier', not 'Staffie'. Deprioritise french bulldog and border collie as bed keywords — the search intent is not there.
