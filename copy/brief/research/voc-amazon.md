## Recurring phrases

- **absolutely loves it / she absolutely loves it** The single most repeated construction in the corpus — appears in a large share of 5-star reviews. 'Absolutely' is the default intensifier, not 'really' or 'very'.
- **won't get out of it / hasn't been out of the bed since it arrived / doesn't get out of it** Appears repeatedly as the proof-of-purchase moment. This is how owners describe success — not comfort scores, but refusal to leave.
- **took to it straight away / took to it immediately / took to it like the proverbial duck** Very common. The fear behind it is that the dog will reject the bed, so instant acceptance is worth reporting.
- **good as new / still as good as new / looks as knew [sic]** Recurs in longevity reviews at 1, 3, 5, 7 and 12 years. The exact phrase owners reach for when defending the price.
- **worth the money / well worth the money / worth every penny** The standard UK value verdict. 'Worth the money' appears more often than 'worth every penny' in actual reviews.
- **still going strong** Used at the 2-year, 5-year, 7-year and 10-year marks. A durability idiom, always paired with a time period.
- **went flat / gone flat / lost their shape / loose their sturdyness** The universal description of old-bed failure. 'Flat' is the word, not 'compressed' or 'lost support'.
- **old boy / poor old boy / my old lurcher / my girl / girly dog** Pet names for senior dogs. 'Old boy' and 'poor old boy' are the male form; females are 'my girl', 'girly dog', 'our greyhound girl', 'our lovely tri-pawed girl'. 'Fur baby' did NOT appear once — but 'my baby', 'our spoilt baby' and 'our little Princess' did.
- **getting on in years / ageing bones / starting to need that extra bit of support / coming up to 13** How owners name ageing without saying 'senior'. They almost never use the word 'senior' themselves — retailers do.
- **stiff / stiff hips / difficult to walk right away after getting up / more mobile in the morning** The functional benefit language. The observable event is getting up, and the time is morning or after a long sleep.
- **easy to wash / washes well / the cover comes off and washes** Mentioned in a majority of reviews of all star ratings, positive and negative. It is the second thing they check after the dog liking it.
- **I wish they'd sell a replacement cover / spare cover** Requested independently in at least six separate reviews across four products. The most common unmet demand in the entire corpus.
- **the zip / zips have come apart / zip broke after the first wash** Zips and Velcro are named specifically and repeatedly as the failure point.
- **definitely NOT waterproof / it isn't definitely waterproof** Owners test waterproof claims immediately and report failure in capitals.
- **had to buy another one / ordered another one / I've just ordered another** The second-bed narrative — usually because another dog or the cat claimed it. Owners report this proudly, as proof.
- **stopped going on the sofa / hasn't been on the couch once / sleeps in her own bed now** A major un-asked-for benefit. Owners buy for the dog's joints and report getting their furniture and their own bed back.
- **vacuum packed / came tightly rolled / hard as a rock / took about 36 hours to reach its final shape** The unboxing-panic moment. Several reviewers describe nearly returning it before it expanded.
- **can't fault it / I simply cannot fault it** The British superlative — praise expressed as absence of complaint.
- **a God send** Used for post-surgery and injury recovery purchases.
- **stretch out / stretch right across / stretch themselves** Sighthound and lab owners judge size by whether the dog can fully stretch out, not by weight charts.
- **walkies** Used unselfconsciously by UK owners in reviews about wet dogs and crates.

## Themes

### The old bed going flat is the actual trigger event — not the dog's age

What owners say: "I have wasted hundreds over the years on shitty dog beds that either went flat, or were easily torn up, or difficult to wash" (Edel); "his last one looked like stig of the dump!"; "they over the months loose their sturdyness"; "all of them got shredded or lost their shape in a few months time". On the Horse & Hound forum the criterion is stated flatly: "about three years old and not gone flat at all".

Why it matters: Open on the failed bed, not on the dog's diagnosis. The buyer arrives already angry at money wasted, with a specific mental image: a flat, shabby, smelly bed in the corner. PawLunova's 5-year no-flatten foam guarantee is the direct answer to a grievance owners voice unprompted — lead with it and quantify it against their lived experience ('most beds are shabby in 18 months'). Anchor the £80-£200 price against 'hundreds wasted over the years', not against cheaper beds.

### The observable symptom is getting up, and the observable window is the morning

What owners say: "My 10 year old, 35 kg dog was finding it difficult to walk right away after getting up from her old bed"; "so much more mobile in the morning after a good nights sleep"; "gets up without the stiffness she can sometimes have after a long sleep"; "she can no longer jump up to her spot on the sofa"; "She had new lease of life today on her walk."

Why it matters: Owners do not describe 'joint health'. They describe a five-second event they watch every morning: the dog trying to stand. Write the benefit as that moment — the first stretch, the walk to the back door, the sound of nails on the floor without the pause first. This is the single highest-emotion, highest-specificity hook available and almost no competitor copy uses it.

### First-night adoption is the proof owners report, because rejection is the fear they carry

What owners say: "our dog won't get out of it anymore"; "he has not been out of the bed since it arrived this afternoon"; "the second she saw t come out of the box she jumped all ver it"; "took to it like the proverbial duck"; "At first she was suspicious of a new one"; and the failure case: "our dog would not use it until we replaced the foam."

Why it matters: The unspoken risk in a £150 dog bed is that the dog ignores it. Every buyer has a bed in the loft the dog never used. The 100-night home trial is the exact antidote and should be framed in their words — 'if he won't get in it, send it back' — not as a policy line. Put it near the price, not in a footer.

### The cover fails long before the foam, and owners want a spare they can buy

What owners say: "the bed itself remains perfect" but "extra covers can not be purchased"; "a spare cover is needed when the original one is being washed and dried" (5 years in, still 5 stars); "I'd buy 3/4 to rotate them during the year"; "the zips are very small and have come apart"; "the zip broke in mine after the first wash"; "the fabric containing the stuffing was weak like tissue paper and disintegrated in her hands."

Why it matters: This is the loudest unmet demand in the whole corpus and the number one reason a 5-star owner still marks a product down. Every product page should state the cover spec concretely (zip type, wash temperature, tumble-dry) and say whether a spare cover is available. If PawLunova sells spare covers, that fact alone is a competitive weapon worth putting in the description and the Shopping feed title. If it does not, state washability in terms that pre-empt the fear: 'washes at 40 and back on the bed the same day'.

### Washing is not a feature — it is the daily reality of the purchase

What owners say: "easy to wash the outer layer"; "I cover it with a single duvet cover folded in two for ease of shaking to remove the dog hairs"; "so easy to remove the toppers which wash and dry quickly"; "the base of bed comes away in wash"; "the Velcro came loose in several places"; "with any dog bed once you start washing them you can only get a good 18 months to 2 years out of them".

Why it matters: Owners have already invented workarounds (duvet covers, towels, rugs over the bed) because they do not trust covers to survive. Beat that by being specific: what comes off, at what temperature, how fast it dries, whether it goes back on easily. 'Machine washable' is worth nothing — 'zip-off cover, 40°C, back on in an hour' is worth the sale.

### Sizing is judged by whether the dog can stretch out — and bolsters eat the usable area

What owners say: "Greyhound like to stretch themselves..."; "she stretches right acriss"; "I would recommend a bigger bed for Labs, as the 100cm was a bit short for my dog"; "Worried it was a bit small for my lanky 12 year old terrier cross"; "Our dog can stretch out completely on this, or put his head on the higher part, or curl up on a ball against the higher part." Buying guides confirm the trap: usable sleeping area is smaller than external dimensions because of thick bolsters.

Why it matters: Publish internal sleeping dimensions, not just external — and name breeds against sizes (lurcher, greyhound, lab, GSD, cocker, Frenchie, terrier cross). Sizing anxiety is a live pre-purchase objection that costs conversions and drives returns. It is also strong SEO: 'large orthopaedic dog bed for greyhound / labrador / german shepherd' matches how they actually search.

### The vacuum-pack unboxing nearly loses the sale

What owners say: "When it arrived rolled in a tight cigar shape it was hard as a rock. I unrolled it and thought.. no way this is awful. Give it overnight to fluff up and it's amazing."; "I was a little worried when I unpacked it, it was vacuum packed and a bit flat. After a few hours however it's nice and thick"; "The bed came tightly rolled and took about 36 hours to reach its final shape."

Why it matters: Multiple owners came within minutes of returning a bed they ended up loving. Pre-empt it on the product page and in the post-purchase email: 'It arrives rolled and will feel hard as a rock. Give it 24-36 hours.' This converts a refund into a five-star review and costs nothing.

### Firm feels wrong to the owner and right to the dog

What owners say: "They feel surprisingly firm to me however the dogs choose to sleep in these rather than on the settee!"; "It is surprisingly heavy and firm, but moulds quickly to give support - I could sleep on it myself!"; "It's firm, but soft enough to be comfortable." Counter-current: one owner deliberately bought soft because "my hips hurt with a firm mattress", and another returned a bed because "foam is too soft our dog would not use it".

Why it matters: Owners press the bed with their hand and judge it by human standards. Explain firmness as a deliberate orthopaedic choice in their language — a firm mattress holds the hips up, a soft one lets them sink — and tell them it will feel firmer than they expect. This turns the most common tactile objection into a credibility signal.

### They buy for the dog's joints and get their sofa and their own bed back

What owners say: "She used to sleep with me and my partner however since this has arrived she actually sleeps in her own bed now!"; "hasn't been on the couch once!"; "Since having this bed she stopped going on our sofa"; "Before this bed she wanted to go out 2 times during the night. Now she... sleeps the whole night through."

Why it matters: A secondary benefit owners discover and then rave about, which no competitor promises up front. It is a legitimate second buying reason for a spouse who is not the dog person: cleaner sofa, uninterrupted sleep. Use as a secondary bullet, never as the lead.

### Proof is denominated in years, and trust is broken by quality decline

What owners say: "I've had it for over 12 years!... it has lasted all 3 of my boys"; "I bought one 6 years ago and still have it!"; "I bought this bed about 7 years ago for my then elderly Springer who is sadly no longer with us now... it is still going strong". Against that: "the newer ones are rubbish"; "The quality has reduced a lot. The base is thinner"; "it's not what it used to be"; "I recently purchased a 3rd large and it's very disappointing it's not as well made as the others".

Why it matters: Repeat buyers are the most vocal and most punishing segment. The 5-year no-flatten guarantee is exactly the promise this corpus is begging for — but it must be stated as a consequence ('if it flattens, we replace it'), with the number in the Shopping feed title and the meta description. Consistency claims ('same foam, same spec, every batch') directly counter the top trust objection in the category.

### The rescue, the tripod, the post-op dog — the emotional ceiling of this category

What owners say: "he is 9 years old and was kept in a cage for 8 of them and we were told we should put him down... as soon as I unpacked it and got it ready he was lying on it and for the first time in his life he was comfortable. It's well made and well cushioned. I hope it treats him well."; "Bought this for my lovely tri-pawed girl. Memory foam supports tired joints."; "he was having tendon repair surgery on both back legs so wanted something sturdy and comfy"; "She had a cruciate ligament injury and this bed had beed a GOD send!"

Why it matters: These are the reviews other buyers read to the end. The emotional register that works is plain and understated — 'for the first time in his life he was comfortable' — never florid marketing sentiment. Match that register: short declarative sentences about a specific dog in a specific state. Overwriting will read as fake to this audience.

### Nest and bolster beds are bought for security and for the headrest, not just warmth

What owners say: "She is so comfortable and cosy on it and sleeps much more as she feels really secure."; "he loves to rest his head on something so the padding around the edge is perfect"; "especially since they can rest their chins on it"; "She takes it between her two big paws, burrows her head into it and will lie there, intertwined with her blanket in absolute contentment."; "he likes to dig himself a little 'nest'"; "he loved the deep, soft raised walls which offers that nesting quality so many dogs prefer".

Why it matters: For nest and bolster lines the benefit is psychological (security, den instinct) plus a concrete physical one (a raised edge to rest the head and neck on, which matters for arthritic necks and for breathing). Note the tension to resolve in copy: high bolsters help the head but block entry for a stiff dog — say explicitly which side is low or how high the step-in is.

### Waterproof claims are tested on day one and failure is reported in capitals

What owners say: "Very Hard and definitely NOT waterproof"; "it isn't definitely waterproof (this since day 1)"; "I was a little disappointed with how much water and stains the cover will let in"; "I just wish the outer material was waterproof as the dogs are often wet when they go to the crates after their walkies"; "despite multiple accidents from her incontinent 13-year-old Spaniel".

Why it matters: Be surgically precise on waterproofing: waterproof liner under a washable cover is not a waterproof cover, and owners will punish any blur between the two. State what is waterproof, what is water-resistant, and what happens in an incontinence accident — because the ageing-dog buyer is dealing with exactly that and nobody says it out loud.

### Delivery and the unboxing day are part of the review

What owners say: "Thanks Zooplus & DPD with tracking info every moment & an hour delivery slot on the day"; "Bed arrived in 3 days in good condition and within minutes of unwrapping, Ollie jumped right in"; "Delivery was fast and reliable too"; "it arrived without a mattress"; "To figure out how to assemble the product, you're on your own."

Why it matters: UK buyers narrate the delivery as part of the product story. State UK mainland delivery timing concretely on the page, and if a bed needs assembly or expansion time, say so before they buy. 'Arrived in 3 days and he was in it within minutes' is the story you want them able to tell.

## Style notes

HOW THESE BUYERS ACTUALLY WRITE — copy the register, not just the words.

SENTENCE SHAPE. Short, flat declaratives, often run together with commas or ellipses instead of full stops: "Bed arrived on time. Our Laoise loved it from the very first minute." Sentences average 8-16 words. Long reviews are strings of short clauses, not built paragraphs. Ampersands are common ("lovely & comfy", "nice & thick"). Typos are everywhere and never apologised for ("acriss", "knew" for "new", "definitly", "beed a GOD send", "wven"). Emphasis is done with CAPITALS on one word ("My dogs LOVE this", "She absolutely LOVES her new bed", "definitely NOT waterproof") and with multiple exclamation marks. Emoji appear in recent reviews (smileys, 😂, 🎉) but sparingly in the older UK ones.

BRITISH SPELLING AND IDIOM — use all of these. orthopaedic (never orthopedic — though buyers themselves slip into both, e.g. "orthapedic"), cosy, favourite, colour, moulds, whilst, settee, couch and sofa used interchangeably, hoovering, walkies, garden (never "yard"), kennels, caravan, hols, the hearth, "in the bin", "stig of the dump", "a God send", "good as new", "can't fault it", "took to it straight away", "worth the money", "still going strong", "gone flat", "getting on in years", "on our hols".

HOW THEY INTRODUCE A DOG. Age, then breed, then sometimes weight — as credentials establishing why their opinion counts: "my 12 year old chocolate labrador Ollie Bob", "My 10 year old, 35 kg dog", "my lanky 12 year old terrier cross", "my Cairn Terrier, who is now 9", "my old lurcher... coming up to 13", "my 16 year old sheepdog", "my Sheltie, Leo who is 14 years". Names are used freely and without explanation: Ollie Bob, Archie, Molly, Leo, Gemma, Fero, Kira, Sam, Uncle Barney.

PET NAMES THEY ACTUALLY USE. "old boy", "poor old boy", "my girl", "girly dog", "our greyhound girl", "my baby", "our spoilt baby", "our little Princess", "my boys", "tri-pawed girl", "doggy", "pup". NOT USED ANYWHERE IN THIS CORPUS: "fur baby", "pupper", "doggo", "pawrent", "furkid". Do not write those. "Fur baby" in particular will read as American and fake to this audience.

WHAT THEY NEVER SAY. No marketing abstractions: no "pressure-relieving sleep system", "therapeutic", "premium sleep solution", "elevate your dog's wellbeing", "revolutionary", "game-changer", "pawsome". No density figures, no ILD ratings, no clinical claims. They never say "senior dog" — retailers say that; owners say "getting on in years", "my old boy", "ageing bones", "starting to need that extra bit of support". They never say "joint health"; they say "stiff", "her hips", "getting up". They do not use superlatives about themselves or about the brand — praise is delivered as the absence of fault ("I simply cannot fault it") or as the dog's behaviour ("she won't get out of it").

HOW THEY EXPRESS EMOTION. Understated and concrete. The most moving line in the whole corpus is plain: "for the first time in his life he was comfortable." Grief is mentioned in passing, without dwelling — "my then elderly Springer who is sadly no longer with us now", "we had to say goodbye to him just over a year ago". Copy that tries to out-feel these people will fail. Match them: state the small physical fact and let the reader supply the emotion.

HOW THEY JUDGE VALUE. Always against elapsed time and against past waste, never against competitor RRP. "I've had it for over 12 years!"; "I have wasted hundreds over the years on shitty dog beds"; "I've seen beds 4 x the price that aren't as good as this one"; "with any dog bed once you start washing them you can only get a good 18 months to 2 years out of them". The persuasive unit is years-per-pound, not features-per-pound.

STRUCTURE OF A CREDIBLE REVIEW — mirror this shape in the product copy itself. (1) The dog: age, breed, problem. (2) The old bed and why it failed. (3) The arrival / unboxing moment. (4) What the dog did in the first hour. (5) The washing verdict. (6) The value verdict with a time period attached. (7) One honest reservation. That seventh beat matters: near-universally, even 5-star UK reviewers add a caveat ("the only down side is...", "I just wish..."). Copy that admits one limitation will read as more credible to this buyer than copy that admits none.

EVIDENCE CAVEAT. Amazon.co.uk returned HTTP 503 on every fetch attempt (three product-review URLs tried), so no quote here is sourced from Amazon. The verbatim corpus above is UK and EU Zooplus customer reviews (zooplus.co.uk and zooplus.com feedback pages, ~5,000 reviews across six orthopaedic/memory-foam/nest/bolster/outdoor products) plus a UK Horse & Hound owners' forum thread and one in-home testing round-up. Quotes attributed to owners on dogbed360.co.uk ("finally stopped crying when getting up after switching to this bed" etc.) were deliberately EXCLUDED from the verbatim list — that site is an affiliate round-up and the attributions could not be traced to a real review, so they should not be reused as customer voice.
