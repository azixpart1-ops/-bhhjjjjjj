# PawLunova Copy Brief
## The single source of truth for all 64 product descriptions

**Version 1.0 · For eight writers · Read all of it before writing one word**

This brief exists so that eight people writing 64 descriptions produce one brand and one customer. Every rule here is drawn from real UK customer language, competitor teardowns, live Google policy and UK regulator rulings. Where a rule has a source, it is named. Where a claim has a legal ceiling, that ceiling is stated.

**One thing first, about the goal.** The instruction behind this project was to write copy that sells hard, emotionally and psychologically, and to win Google Shopping. Those two goals constrain each other in exactly one place: fabricated pressure. Fake urgency, fake stock counts, fake reviews and medical promises are not merely risky here — under the DMCC Act 2024 fake reviews are strict-liability, and under Google's Misrepresentation policy the penalty is Merchant Center suspension "upon detection and without prior warning", which deletes the Shopping goal outright. So we are not using those levers. We do not need them. The evidence in this research is emphatic that what actually moves this specific buyer is **specificity, recognition and removal of risk** — and almost no competitor is doing it. That is the manipulation, properly understood: we are going to describe this customer's own living room back to her more accurately than she expects, and then take away every reason to hesitate. That converts harder than a countdown timer and it cannot be taken off us.

# 1. THE CUSTOMER, IN THEIR OWN WORDS

## 1.1 Who she is

She is a UK dog owner, usually 45+, usually female, usually the person in the house who notices things about the dog first. She has owned this dog for most of its life. She is making an £80–£200 decision she has been circling for a few weeks.

She almost never introduces herself. She introduces the dog — age first, then breed, then sometimes weight, as credentials that establish why her opinion counts: *"my 12 year old chocolate labrador Ollie Bob"*, *"My 10 year old, 35 kg dog"*, *"my lanky 12 year old terrier cross"*, *"my Sheltie, Leo who is 14 years"*, *"nearly 10-year old smallish collie (she's 17.5kg)"*.

She does not say "senior dog". Retailers say that. She says:

> *"Dave doesn't really do beds, but at 9 years old, he's starting to feel the effects of an overly exuberant youth"* — Gemma, Omlet Topology reviews

> *"my Cairn Terrier, who is now 9, and starting to need that extra bit of support and comfort"* — Libby, Zooplus UK, 12/03/23

> *"This was a good buy, it is thick, lightweight and comfortable for my dogs ageing bones"* — Zooplus, 22/02/18

## 1.2 What she is actually frightened of

Not arthritis in the abstract. Five specific things, in this order of emotional weight.

**(a) Watching him get old.** This is the floor under everything else, and it is stated more plainly than any copywriter would dare:

> *"It is horrible to see them get old."* — NormasJeans, Mumsnet, *Older dog arthritis adaptations*

> *"I just love the old boy and want him to be happy and not in pain."* — PlantDoctor, Mumsnet

> *"He obviously wants to be with us but doesn't feel up to it."* — PlantDoctor, Mumsnet

That third quote is the whole category in eleven words. The loss is not mobility. The loss is *company*.

> *"staggering around and slipping and sliding"* … *"He's much worse on the laminate flooring."* — peppapigkeepsthemquiet, Mumsnet

**(c) Losing the sofa, and with it the closeness.**

Read that last one again. **She has already accepted that a dog bed dies in 18 months.** That is the expectation we are beating.

**(e) That he simply will not use it.** This is the specific fear that stops the £149 purchase:

# 2. THE VOICE

## 2.1 Hard mechanics

| Rule | Setting |
|---|---|
| **Person** | Second person for the owner ("you"), third for the dog ("they/them" by default, "he/she" when a specific scenario needs it) |
| **Tense** | Present. "They lower themselves, shift, and shift again." Never "will transform", never "has been engineered" |
| **Sentence length** | Average 8–16 words. Maximum 25. One idea per sentence |
| **Paragraph length** | Maximum 3 sentences. Most are 2 |
| **Exclamation marks** | Zero. Not one, anywhere, in any of the 64 |
| **Em dashes** | Avoid. Use a full stop. Our customer writes in full stops and commas |
| **Rhetorical questions as headings** | Allowed, in customer voice only ("Can I actually wash it?"). Never accusatory ("Still letting your dog sleep on the floor?") |
| **Spelling** | UK throughout: orthopaedic, colour, favourite, cosy, moulds, draught, metres, centimetres, 40°C, £ |
| **The US spelling** | "orthopedic" appears exactly once per product, in a metafield or search-boost string only. Never in visible body copy, H1 or feed title |
| **Numbers** | Always digits. "24cm", "5 years", "100 nights", "8p a night", "91 × 69cm". Never "twenty-four centimetres" |
| **Dimensions format** | `91 × 69 × 24cm` — multiplication sign, spaces, unit once at the end |

## 2.2 How to refer to the dog

- **Default:** "your dog", "they", "them"
- **When you need a scene:** "a big dog", "an older dog", "a stiff dog", "a dog who leans"
- **Permitted affectionate register, used sparingly and only where the product is aimed at ageing dogs:** "the old boy", "your old girl"
- **Breeds by name** in the sizing block and body copy: lurcher, greyhound, whippet, labrador, cocker spaniel, German shepherd, dachshund, Staffy, golden retriever, Great Dane, terrier cross, collie, springer
- **Never:** fur baby, furbaby, pupper, doggo, pawrent, furkid, four-legged friend, furry friend, canine companion, pet parent. **Zero instances of any of these appear anywhere in the UK review corpus.** "Fur baby" in particular reads American and will lose the sale.

## 2.3 How to refer to the owner

- "you", always
- Describe what she has **already noticed**, never what she should have done
- Recognition, not qualification. ✅ *"You already know which back leg they favour getting up."* ❌ *"Only serious owners invest in proper support."*

## 2.5 THE BAN LIST — words our customer never uses

Any of these in a draft is an automatic rewrite.

**Marketing abstractions:** premium, luxury, luxurious, revolutionary, game-changer, game-changing, cutting-edge, engineered, ergonomically engineered, innovative, ultimate, elevate, elevates, transform, transforms, indulge, pamper, spoil, treat them to, unlock, discover, introducing, experience the difference, say goodbye to, sleep system, sleep solution, wellbeing solution, pressure-relieving sleep system, next-level, world-class, best-in-class

**Fake intimacy:** fur baby, pawrent, doggo, pupper, pawfect, paw-some, tail-waggingly, furry friend, four-legged friend, canine companion, pet parent, your best friend deserves

**Clinical jargon she never uses:** hindquarters, rear limbs, osteoarthritic degeneration, pressure redistribution, spinal alignment technology, joint pathology, ILD rating, joint health

**Forbidden for legal reasons — see Section 7:** therapeutic, clinically proven, medically proven, vet-approved, vet-recommended, veterinary-grade, orthopaedic-grade, relieves, eases pain, alleviates, soothes, treats, cures, heals, reduces inflammation, improves circulation, speeds recovery, anti-anxiety *(as a claimed effect)*, calms, reduces stress

**Forbidden phrase with a special note:** *"quality of life"*. Our customers do use it — but only in end-of-life conversations. It is contaminated. Keep it out of sales copy entirely.

## 2.6 The credibility rule

**Every sentence containing an evaluative adjective must also contain a number, a measurement, or a named mechanism. Otherwise delete the sentence.**

| ❌ Delete | ✅ Rewrite as |
|---|---|
| premium supportive foam | 24cm of gel memory foam |
| easy to clean | the cover unzips on three sides and washes at 40°C |
| exceptionally durable | a 5-year no-flatten guarantee on the foam core |
| generously sized | 122 × 89cm, around 70% more floor area than a typical 90 × 70cm large bed |
| machine washable | zip-off cover, 40°C, back on the bed the same day |

And: **no abstract noun may be the subject of a sentence.** Not comfort, not luxury, not wellbeing, not quality. Use surfaces, heights, temperatures, times of day.

## 2.7 The one honest flaw

Every product carries **exactly one** admitted limitation, placed *after* the specification block, never before it. It must be a real trade-off with a stated reason, and it must be the flaw a buyer would otherwise discover in someone else's review.

> *"It is heavy. The Large is 7.2kg, which is not a bed you will casually carry upstairs. That weight is the foam density doing its job — a lighter bed is a thinner one."*

> *"It will feel firmer than you expect when you press it with your hand. That is deliberate. A soft bed lets the hips sink; a firm one holds them level."*

> *"Our Large fits a 30kg labrador who sleeps curled. If yours sleeps flat out on their side, order the XL. More beds come back to us for this one reason than any other."*

This is the pratfall effect (Aronson, 1966) and its boundary condition is strict: **the admission only works after competence is established.** Never open with it.

# 3. THE EMOTIONAL ARC

Nine sections. Same order on all 64 products. Each section has one psychological job and one sentence shape that does it.

---

### § 1 — H2 SPEC HOOK
**Job:** Tell a Solution-Aware Google Shopping arrival, in under 12 words, that they are in the right place.
**Shape:** Three fragments. Spec. Spec. Human line.
**Length:** One line.

> `## 24cm deep. Gel memory foam. For the dog who has earned it.`

---

### § 2 — THE OBSERVED BEHAVIOUR
**Job:** Recognition. She must think *"that is my dog"* before she thinks about the product. This is the single highest-value paragraph on the page and the one competitors do not write.
**Shape:** Describe a physical action she has personally watched. Present tense. No product name. 40–70 words.
**Rule:** Name the moment, the sound, the surface — never the feeling.

> *"Watch a big dog lie down on a thin bed. They lower themselves, shift, and shift again, because there is not much between them and the floor."*

> *"They are not being awkward when they wedge themselves against the sofa arm. Leaning is how a lot of dogs switch off."*

---

### § 3 — THE MECHANISM, IN PHYSICAL TERMS
**Job:** Convert the observation into a reason this specific bed answers it. Bridges emotion to spec without a medical claim.
**Shape:** "At [number], [physical consequence]."
**Length:** 1–2 sentences.

> *"At 24cm there is a lot of foam underneath them, so hips and elbows are cushioned however they land."*

---

### § 4 — WHAT THEY GET
**Job:** Scannable proof. She is skimming. Bold lead-in carries the fact, the sentence carries the consequence.
**Shape:** 4–6 bullets. `**Bold noun phrase:** Consequence sentence.`
**Rule:** Every bullet contains a number or a named material.

Close with one line: `**Best for:** [dog type], [dog type], and [owner situation].`

---

### § 5 — WHY IT IS WORTH THE MONEY
**Job:** Convert *"£149 is a lot"* into *"a bit spendy but worth it"*. This is the permission section.
**Shape:** Name the price out loud. Say where it goes. Do the per-night arithmetic across the guarantee. Then remove the risk.
**Length:** 60–90 words.

> *"£149 is a lot for a dog bed, so here is where it goes. [Spec]. [Spec]. Covered by a 5-year no-flatten guarantee, which works out at about 8p a night. And you are not gambling the money — if they have not claimed it within 100 nights, we collect it free from the UK mainland and refund you in full."*

**Per-night formula:** `price ÷ 1825 nights`. Apply to products **£99 and above only**. Below £99 the figure is trivially small and the arithmetic reads as padding.

---

### § 6 — THE HONEST FLAW
**Job:** Credibility through admitted weakness, placed after competence is proved.
**Shape:** One short declarative, then the reason.
**Length:** 1–2 sentences.

---

### § 7 — SIZE AND FIT
**Job:** Kill the single biggest add-to-basket blocker and the single biggest driver of returns.
**Must contain, without exception:**
- A dimensions table
- **Internal sleeping area** where a bolster eats the footprint — and say so
- Named UK breeds against each size
- The measuring instruction, verbatim every time: *"Measure your dog lying on their side, nose to base of tail, and add 15 to 20cm."*
- The human offer: *"Not sure? Message us their breed and weight and we will tell you honestly which one fits."*

---

### § 8 — EVERYDAY CARE + THE VET LINE
**Job:** Answer *"can I actually wash it?"* and close the medical-claim door.
**Shape:** Instructions, then the standing vet sentence, unchanged on all 64:

> *"If your dog is in pain, recovering or finding it hard to move, speak to your vet about the right resting surface. A bed is for comfort. It is not a treatment."*

That paragraph is not optional and is not to be reworded. It is the compliance anchor for the whole page.

---

### § 9 — RISK REVERSAL + CTA
**Job:** Last objection down. Finish on an instruction, not a feeling.
**Shape:** Trial. Guarantee. Delivery. One-line command.

> `**Choose their size and put 24cm between them and the floor.**`

**Closing CTA rule:** always an imperative containing the product's single defining spec. Never "Shop now". Never "Give them the comfort they deserve".

---

**Word budget for the whole body: 350–550 words.** Do not pad. Berkeley ranks page one on 595 words with seven reviews; Lords & Labradors ranks on 34 words. Length is not the lever.

# 4. TEN OPENING-LINE PATTERNS

Each product uses **one**. Rotate across the catalogue so no two products in a family open the same way. Every pattern is derived from something a real customer said.

---

**1 — THE WATCHED MOMENT**
*Describe a five-second physical event she sees every day.*
> Watch them get up after a long sleep. There is a pause before the back legs commit, and it happens on hard floor more than anywhere else.

**2 — THE GRAVEYARD**
*Name the pile of failed beds. From "I have wasted hundreds over the years on shitty dog beds".*
> Somewhere in your house there is a bed they never used. Most owners have two.

**3 — THE 18-MONTH EXPECTATION**
*Attack the low bar she has already accepted. From "you can only get a good 18 months to 2 years out of them".*
> Most dog beds are shabby inside two years and everyone has quietly accepted that. This one is guaranteed not to flatten for five.

**4 — THE MISFIT**
*For XL and giant lines. From "my black labrador is male, 44kg. He does not fit well on the largest size".*
> If you own a big dog you already know that most large beds are large for a spaniel. Yours ends up half on and half off, back legs on the floor.

**5 — THE LEAN**
*For bolster and nest lines. From "she likes to sleep on her back so quite enjoys a raised edge to prop herself up against".*
> They are not being awkward when they wedge themselves against the sofa arm. Leaning is how a lot of dogs switch off.

**6 — THE PILLOW**
*For bolsters. From "the raised areas allow him to rest his head without stretching this vulnerable area".*
> Owners buy a bolster thinking it is a wall. The dog uses it as a pillow.

**7 — THE TWO-WAY PULL**
*For anything that has to live in the lounge. From "it looks in keeping with our furniture".*
> Two things pull against each other when you buy a dog bed. The one that looks right in your living room is thin and flat inside. The one with proper foam in it is grey ripstop and ends up behind a chair.

**8 — THE ACCIDENT, NAMED WITHOUT SQUEAMISHNESS**
*For waterproof lines. From "senior dog bed for incontinence" — a real, high-volume, entirely unserved search.*
> Old dogs have accidents, and nobody writes about it. A washable cover is not the answer on its own — what matters is whether anything reaches the foam.

**9 — THE FIRST SIXTY SECONDS**
*From "As soon as I got this bed out, she jumped straight on it!" — the most repeated narrative beat across every competitor brand.*
> Unroll it and give it a day to come up to full height. Most dogs are on it before the box is folded.

**10 — THE SLEEPING STYLE**
*From PDSA: "Allow your dog to choose whether to snuggle in an enclosed bed or stretch out on a flat mattress."*
> There are curlers and there are stretchers. A curler on a flat mattress spends the night hunting for an edge that is not there.

---

**Opening-line bans:** never open with a question to the reader. Never open with the product name. Never open with foam density. Never open with "Introducing". Never open with the dog's mortality.

# 5. PROOF AND RISK-REVERSAL LANGUAGE

These are **fixed blocks**. Copy them exactly. Do not paraphrase, do not improve, do not shorten. They are legally load-bearing.

## 5.1 The 100-night trial

**It is never called a "free trial."** CAP rule 3.26 prohibits "free trial" for satisfaction-or-money-back offers; the ASA upheld against HealthMail Ltd (2005) on exactly this. It is a **home trial**.

**Full block (§9, all 64 products), verbatim:**

> Try it at home for 100 nights from delivery. If it is not right, we arrange free UK mainland collection and a full refund. One trial per household; damage and other exclusions apply.

**Short form (§5, near the price):**

> And you are not gambling the money. If they have not claimed it within 100 nights, we collect it free from the UK mainland and refund you in full.

**Customer-voice form (FAQ answer to "What if my dog just won't use it?"):**

> Then send it back. That is what the 100 nights are for. We collect it free from the UK mainland and refund you in full — you do not need to have kept the box.

> ⚠️ Only publish the box clause if operations genuinely accept returns without original packaging. Verify before it goes on 64 pages.

## 5.2 The 5-year no-flatten guarantee

**Full block, verbatim:**

> The foam core also has a 5-year no-flatten guarantee under normal domestic use, subject to the guarantee terms; cover wear and pet damage are not included. [Read the full returns and guarantee terms](/policies/refund-policy). This guarantee is in addition to your statutory rights and does not affect them.

**Why the limitations sit *in* the copy:** CAP requires that ads "must not mislead by omitting significant limitations to guarantees, particularly those affecting consumer rights." Exclusions in a linked PDF only is the failure mode the ASA rules against.

**Customer-voice form, using her own words for the failure:**

> Beds go flat. That is the complaint everyone has and it is the one thing we put in writing: if the foam core loses its shape inside five years under normal domestic use, we replace it.

**Never write "no-flatten" without a definition somewhere on the page.** An undefined performance guarantee is an omitted-limitation problem waiting to happen.

## 5.3 Delivery

**Verbatim:**

> Free standard UK delivery on orders over £40. [Check delivery timings and any product-specific lead time](/policies/shipping-policy).

Where a Highlands, islands or Northern Ireland surcharge applies, it appears **next to** the free-delivery claim, never at checkout. Drip pricing is prohibited outright under the DMCC Act (CMA207).

## 5.4 Sizing help

**Verbatim in every § 7:**

> Measure your dog lying on their side, nose to base of tail, and add 15 to 20cm. Not sure? Message us their breed and weight and we will tell you honestly which one fits.

Where a bolster reduces the usable area, add:

> These are outside measurements and the bolster takes up some of that.

## 5.5 The vet line

**Verbatim in every § 8, unchanged:**

> If your dog is in pain, recovering or finding it hard to move, speak to your vet about the right resting surface. A bed is for comfort. It is not a treatment.

## 5.6 Proof we do NOT have — hard stop

Until real reviews exist, **no writer may produce**: a star rating, a review count, a testimonial, a named customer quote, "loved by X UK dog owners", "bestseller", "X people are viewing this", "only N left", a countdown, or `aggregateRating` / `review` JSON-LD.

The competitor quotes throughout this brief are **research material for register only**. Reproducing any of them on pawlunova.co.uk would be a banned practice under the DMCC Act and grounds for Merchant Center suspension.

**What we use instead, and it is enough:** the trial, the guarantee, the measured spec, the named breeds, and the honest flaw. Berkeley Dog Beds ranks page one in the UK with **seven** reviews and a spec-dense 595-word description. Specification is the proof when endorsement is not available.

## 7.1 Health and medical claims

| # | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Never claim the bed **treats, cures, relieves, eases, soothes or reduces** pain or any condition — directly or indirectly. | CAP, *Health: Pain*: "Marketers of devices that are not appropriately certified as a medical device should not make claims to treat, relieve or reduce pain (directly or indirectly)." |
| 2 | Never name **arthritis, arthritic, hip dysplasia, dysplasia, IVDD, joint pain, inflammation** as something the bed acts upon. | CAP, *Health: Pain* — naming a condition requiring medical supervision engages rule 12.2. |
| 3 | Never claim the bed **reduces anxiety, stress, separation anxiety or nervousness.** | **ASA upheld against A&T International t/a Bella&Toby (A22-1169887)** — a dog bed, for this exact claim. "DON'T LET YOUR PET SUFFER WHILE YOU'RE AT WORK" was struck down. Closest precedent to this store. |
| 4 | No medicinal claim about an animal is available **at any evidence level**, because the product is unlicensed. | CAP, *Vetting claims about products for animal health*: "Medicinal claims made for an unlicensed product break the rules, regardless of any evidence you may hold." |
| 5 | Never claim **improved circulation, reduced inflammation, faster recovery** or immune benefit. | CAP, *Adjustable beds and chairs*. |
| 6 | "Orthopaedic" is permitted as a **category descriptor**. It must never be dressed in proof language — no "medically proven", "clinically proven", "orthopaedic-grade", "veterinary-grade", "vet-approved", "designed by orthopaedic specialists". | **ASA upheld against Origin Sleep UK (A24-1253221)** — read "Orthopaedic Back Support" as a claim of medically-proven support requiring clinical trials. |
| 7 | No numeric or comparative performance claim against other beds ("3x more support", "twice the pressure relief") without held evidence. | Origin Sleep ruling, CAP rule 3.33. |
| 8 | **The permitted lane:** positional, temporary comfort. Describe firmness, depth, entry height, weight distribution as construction facts, and let the owner draw the inference. | CAP, *Adjustable beds and chairs*: products may claim "positional, temporary relief or comfort" — but must not imply treatment of a condition or relief beyond temporary. |
| 9 | The standing vet paragraph (§5.5) appears unchanged on all 64. | Our own compliance anchor. |
| 10 | Never cite the "80% of senior dogs have arthritis" figure. UK VetCompass data on 455,557 dogs found **2.5%** annual period prevalence. | Anderson et al., *Scientific Reports* 2018. Either cite that properly or use no prevalence figure. |

# 9. OPEN ITEMS — flag to the client, do not invent

1. **Melbreak (`the-loweswater-orthopaedic-bolster-dog-bed`) has a price contradiction.** The variant is **£155.00**; the body copy says *"£195 is a lot for a dog bed"* and computes 11p a night from £195. 2. **`google_product_category` on Ennerdale is set to `2`.** Must be `4434`. Audit all 64.
3. **Spare covers.** The most-requested unmet need in the entire 5,000-review corpus, raised independently in six reviews across four products. 4. **Return packaging.** Confirm whether returns are accepted without the original box before the clause *"you do not need to have kept the box"* ships on 64 pages.
