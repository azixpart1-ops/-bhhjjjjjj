Before you buy another bed

Dogs don't tell you the bed has stopped working.
They show you.

01

They circle for a long time before they settle.

Circling is normal. Circling, re-circling and still shifting ten minutes later usually isn't. It means no position on that bed takes the pressure off.

02

They've started sleeping on the hard floor instead.

A dog choosing cold tiles over their own bed is telling you something. Once foam has bottomed out, the floor is genuinely the firmer, more supportive option.

03

Getting up takes a beat longer than it used to.

The pause before the first step. The slower rise in the morning. It arrives so gradually that most owners only really notice it looking back.

Ageing isn't something a bed can undo. What a bed can change is how much work it takes to lie down — and to get back up again.

Every PawLunova memory foam core is 50kg/m³. That number is what decides whether a bed is still doing its job in year five. It's also why we're willing to guarantee the foam for five of them.

Before you decide

The questions we get asked most.

Measure your dog from nose to base of tail while they're lying down, then add 15–20cm. If they're between two sizes, size up — dogs that sprawl need the extra length more than you'd think. Our size and firmness guide has the breed-by-breed detail. And if you get it wrong, the 100-night trial covers it.

It's the density of the foam — how much material is packed into every cubic metre. Density isn't the same as firmness: a low-density foam can feel firm on day one and still collapse by month six. High-density foam keeps its structure under repeated weight, which is why a 50kg/m³ core is still supporting your dog's hips and elbows years later. It's also why we can put a 5-year guarantee on it.

Our beds are vet-informed and built to distribute weight so that pressure isn't concentrated on hips, elbows and shoulders — which is what makes lying down and getting back up harder for dogs with joint wear. A bed isn't a treatment, and it won't replace veterinary care, so please keep talking to your vet. What it can do is change the surface they spend fourteen hours a day on.
